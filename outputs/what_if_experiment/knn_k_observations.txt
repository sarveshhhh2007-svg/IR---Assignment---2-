OPTIMAL CONFIGURATION: K = 3 (Achieving Spam F1 = 0.8936)

ANALYSIS & THEORETICAL INTERPRETATION:
1. Bias-Variance Trade-off:
   Smaller values of K (e.g. K=3) create more sensitive and localized decision
   boundaries, making the classifier responsive to sparse minority spam clusters
   but potentially more susceptible to noise.

2. Majority Class Overwhelm:
   As K increases (e.g. K=7, 9), the voting pool expands. Because the dataset has
   an inherent class imbalance (approx. 87% ham vs 13% spam), a larger K causes the
   dominant legitimate class to increasingly outvote spam instances in boundary
   regions unless distance weighting is explicitly applied.

3. Precision vs. Recall Dynamics:
   Empirically, KNN with cosine distance maintains strong precision because nearest
   neighbors in TF-IDF space with specific spam tokens share unambiguous collocations.
   However, recall may fluctuate as higher K values miss borderline spam messages.
